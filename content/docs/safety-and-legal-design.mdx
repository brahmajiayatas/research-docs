The platform should be built around a jurisdiction-aware, rules-driven, safety-first architecture that separates legal information from legal advice and automated assistance from professional decision-making. The system must never allow a general-purpose AI model to independently interpret or invent laws, calculate legal obligations, or provide definitive legal conclusions.

The core architecture should follow:

> Jurisdiction → Verified Legal Rules → Decision Tree → Case Facts → Calculation Engine → AI Explanation

rather than:

> User Question → LLM → Legal Answer

## Clear Separation of Functions

The platform should maintain strict boundaries between:

1. **Legal Information** — Provides verified, jurisdiction-specific information from approved legal sources.
2. **Legal Workflow Guidance** — Explains procedural steps, required information, documents, and workflow activities.
3. **Financial Calculations** — Uses deterministic, jurisdiction-configured calculation engines rather than AI-generated calculations.
4. **Negotiation Assistance** — Helps users identify differences, communicate positions, and explore mutually acceptable options.
5. **AI-Generated Suggestions** — Produces neutral summaries, questions, explanations, and draft language clearly identified as AI-assisted.
6. **Professional Legal Advice** — Reserved for qualified attorneys or other authorized professionals.
7. **Final Legal Decisions** — Remain with the spouses, their professional advisors, and ultimately the appropriate legal authority or court.

## Safety and Escalation Framework

A dedicated Safety & Escalation Engine should continuously evaluate information throughout the case. Escalation triggers should include domestic violence, threats, coercion, child-safety concerns, financial concealment, complex business ownership, high-value or international assets, cryptocurrency, complex taxation, retirement or pension interests, significant income disparities, high-conflict communication, custody disagreements, and questions outside the platform's verified knowledge.

When a trigger is detected, the platform should pause, restrict, or modify automated mediation, depending on severity. It should explain why professional involvement may be necessary and provide an appropriate pathway to a qualified mediator, attorney, financial professional, or other specialist.

The platform should also maintain confidence thresholds, source/version tracking, audit logs, human-review checkpoints, and fail-safe controls. If the system cannot verify an applicable rule, it should explicitly state that the information is unavailable rather than generate an answer.

This architecture ensures that AI functions as a controlled assistant within a professionally governed divorce workflow—not as an autonomous legal decision-maker
