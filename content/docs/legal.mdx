## What the product must not do

AI assists the mediation process; it does not decide the outcome, provide unauthorized legal advice, or determine what either spouse should accept.

The system should preserve party self-determination and procedural fairness. For example, California's mediation rules emphasize balanced participation and uncoerced decisions. See [Sources](/sources).

## Documents

The platform should clearly distinguish an **AI-generated draft** from a **professionally reviewed/legal document**. See [Agreement generation](/engines/agreements).

Do not represent an AI-generated document as legally valid merely because the AI produced it.

## Filing

Provide jurisdiction-specific procedural information, while avoiding representations that the platform itself has completed a court filing unless that capability actually exists.

## Jurisdiction review

Before launching in a jurisdiction, have qualified family-law professionals review the decision trees, legal knowledge base, disclosures, escalation rules, generated documents, privacy model, and claims made by the product.

The software architecture can be standardized, but the legal workflow should remain jurisdiction-specific and version-controlled.
